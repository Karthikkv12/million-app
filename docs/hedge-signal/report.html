Buy the dip only when the whole market is scared

I tested the Add / Hold / Trim signal for your LEAPS and long puts on 31.9 years of daily prices. The signal that was live had no edge. One pattern held up across every test, and the desk now runs on that pattern alone.

Re-tested 2026-11-14: parts of this report are corrected or withdrawn — the 1.2× IV gate (hindsight; dropped), the 180-day roll (rolling lost to holding at every days-left), the “Sharpe within 0.03” line and several counts. See docs/hedge-signal-review.md. Holdings are no longer frozen here: the Hedge desk shows the signal for your live positions.

Finding 1

The old signal was worse than doing nothing

After the old Trim call, stocks went up more than after its Add call. It was picking up volatility, not direction. Followed as a policy, it lowered both return and risk-adjusted return.

Finding 2 · the pattern

Why a stock is down decides what happens next

A dip while the whole market is afraid (VIX in the top 20% of its year) has rebounded. A dip while the market is calm is a problem with the stock, and it hasn't. Strength was never a reliable sell: trend, momentum and buying volume all pointed slightly the wrong way.

Stops fail in fear. A close under the 20-day low during market fear came before a +1.8% to +2.7% rebound versus holding. The desk no longer shows a stop while the VIX is high.

Robustness

The Add rule, year by year

  • Buying at the next day’s open instead of the signal’s close: +1.22%, the same.
  • Holds in ETFs (+1.1%), in the 69 crashed stocks (+1.2%) and in the rest (+1.4%), so it is not only survivors bouncing.
  • Beat holding in 159 of 241 separate fear episodes (66%) over 20 days, and 156 over 60 days.

It fails in long bear markets. 2008: −2.9% over 20 days and −6.5% over 60. 2022: −0.3% and −5.1%. The worst single episodes were 17–26% behind holding. None of the 6 filters I tried fixed this out of sample (SPY trend, a deeper VIX threshold, VIX easing off its peak and combinations). With LEAPS, add in pieces.

Finding 3

No timing rule beat holding on risk

Run as a full policy on every stock, each rule traded return for drawdown. Adding on fear dips earned more but went deeper. Cutting on weakness smoothed the ride but cost return. Sharpe stayed within 0.03 of buy-and-hold in both periods.

Second pass · the options themselves

The edge survives theta and IV, if you buy time and hold

LEAPS lose value to time and move with IV, so I re-ran every symbol-day as a simulated long call priced with Black-Scholes: the real price path, a 30-day IV model, a long-dated term structure, T-bill rates, and stress costs of +5 vol points on entry and 2% of the option price each way. That is 808,838 symbol-days for each of 28 setups (90 to 540 days, 0.70 or 0.80Δ, held 20 or 60 days).

The signal’s edge is about 3.5% per 20 days, so it only beats decay on calls with more than about 240 days left. The desk now says add with 365+ days at about 0.80Δ, and shows Roll once a leg loses 4% or more per 20 trading days (about 180 days left). Each leg shows its days left, IV, delta, time value, decay and dollars per day.

The 1.2× IV gate is partly hindsight: IV-normal fear dips were fine in 1995–2014 and weak only in 2015–2026. I kept it because it only removes adds. With a live chain the desk is stricter still and uses the lower of the model’s IV and the market’s.

Limits

What this test cannot tell you

  • Options flow and dealer levels (GEX walls, gamma flip) have no multi-year history, so they are untested. The desk shows them as context and keeps them out of the verdict.
  • Per-stock implied volatility has no history here. The VIX stands in for fear.
  • Option prices are modelled, not real fills. The IV model is validated on five real IV histories and today’s chains, but it misses earnings premiums and ignores dividends and skew. Costs are set conservatively to cover part of that.
  • Survivorship. All 148 symbols still trade. The ETF and crashed-stock checks limit this bias but don’t remove it.
